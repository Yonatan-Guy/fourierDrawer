Diagnostic · camera

Camera permission probe

Watch when the browser actually holds your camera, and when it lets go — and test your own capture-and-send path end to end. Frames stay on your phone until you press Send frame (or arm the timer below); then the current frame is posted to your Telegram bot through this site's Netlify function. Everything it does is on screen: the preview is always visible and every send is logged.

Not a secure context. Cameras only work over HTTPS or on localhost. Opened from a file or over plain HTTP, the request below will fail before it ever reaches a permission prompt.

Things worth trying
  1. Start the camera and look at your phone. The OS indicator appears next to the battery. It is drawn by the system, not by this page.
  2. Switch to another tab or app, wait, come back. Read the trace: if the amber channel stayed high while the blue one dropped, capture continued while you were away.
  3. Press Stop tracks. The indicator should go out within a second. The permission is still granted — only the stream ended.
  4. Press Start again. No second prompt on most browsers, because the grant outlived the stream.
  5. Close the tab, reopen this page, press Start. Whether you are asked again is the difference between a remembered grant and a session-only one. iOS Safari and Chrome differ here.
  6. List devices before granting, then after. Camera names are hidden until a page has been given access — that is a fingerprinting defence.
Delete camtest.html when you're done — while it is deployed, anyone with the link can load it, and if your Telegram env vars are set, anyone who presses Send posts a frame to your bot. Capture is local until you send; sending is the only network call the page makes, and it is always the result of a button or the armed timer.
